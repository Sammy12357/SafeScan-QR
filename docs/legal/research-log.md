# SafeScan legal research log

**Checked:** 2026-11-15 (America/Toronto). **Scope:** primary statutes, regulator guidance, standards bodies, and the relevant providers' own terms. This is an engineering research record for the legal-risk plan, not a conclusion that a law applies to SafeScan or a substitute for counsel's review. Recheck sources before changing public terms, policies, billing, email, or data practices.

## Findings that affect the first implementation phases

| Topic | Primary source | SafeScan implication and limit | Review |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| COPPA coverage and age screen | [FTC COPPA FAQ](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/complying-coppa-frequently-asked-questions), especially sections H.1 and H.3 | A general-audience service may block users under 13. An age question should be neutral and placed when inviting account creation or personal-information submission; an “I am over 12” checkbox is not a neutral screen. Actual knowledge can arise from a user response or a credible report. Whether SafeScan is general audience is a fact-specific judgment; an age gate alone does not settle it. Keep the threshold and the pending identity server controlled, and minimize information collected before the gate. | Attorney for audience classification and response procedure. |
| Amended COPPA Rule | [FTC final rule in the Federal Register](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2025-04-22/html/2025-05904.htm) | The amendments took effect June 23, 2025; most compliance obligations began April 22, 2026, with specified exceptions. The amended Rule also addresses biometric identifiers and retention. Do not treat April 22 as a universal date for every provision. | Attorney if child data is collected or a child-directed audience is possible. |
| Age-verification information | [FTC February 2026 policy statement](https://search.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2026/02/ftc-issues-coppa-policy-statement-incentivize-use-age-verification-technologies-protect-children) | The FTC describes conditional enforcement discretion for age-verification data used only to determine age, promptly deleted, protected, and subject to other conditions. This is not blanket permission to collect extra identity documents, retain birth dates, or send age data to an unreviewed vendor. | Attorney before using third-party age verification. |
| US commercial email | [FTC CAN-SPAM guide](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/can-spam-act-compliance-guide-business) | Commercial mail needs accurate headers and subject, sender location including a valid physical postal address, an easy opt-out, and processing of opt-outs within 10 business days. A transactional classification depends on the message's primary purpose; a waitlist or promotional email should not be assumed transactional. Do not send marketing mail until a real business address and suppression mechanism are configured. | Owner supplies address; attorney reviews message classification and wording. |
| Canadian recipients | [CRTC CASL FAQ](https://crtc.gc.ca/eng/com500/faq500.htm); [CRTC consent guidance](https://crtc.gc.ca/eng/com500/guide.htm) | CASL can cover commercial electronic messages sent from outside Canada to recipients in Canada. Consent, sender identification, and an unsubscribe method are the main requirements. An email asking the recipient to provide express consent may itself be a regulated commercial message; collect opt-in in the web form before any confirmation email. CASL has exemptions and implied-consent categories that require case-specific review. | Attorney for Canadian outreach and exceptions. |
| Mail-provider deliverability | [Gmail sender guidelines](https://support.google.com/mail/answer/81126?hl=en-GB); [Yahoo Sender Hub](https://senders.yahooinc.com/subhub/) | Gmail's bulk threshold is more than 5,000 daily messages to personal Gmail accounts; marketing/subscribed messages at that scale need RFC 8058 one-click unsubscribe plus a visible body link. Yahoo also publishes sender requirements for one-click unsubscribe. Implementing those headers now is sensible, but these are provider policies, not CAN-SPAM's statutory definition of one click. | Engineering verifies actual headers and endpoint behavior. |
| Internet subscriptions | [ROSCA, 15 U.S.C. § 8403](https://uscode.house.gov/us/usc/119-118/t15/s8403/) | For an online negative-option sale, disclose all material terms clearly before obtaining billing information, get express informed consent before charging, and provide a simple way to stop recurring charges. SafeScan should show the true renewal, price, cancellation path, and refund position at the point of purchase and keep a record of consent. A Stripe link alone does not establish that these duties were met. | Owner chooses refund position; attorney approves purchase terms. |
| FTC “Click-to-Cancel” status | [FTC October 2026 Federal Register notice](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2026-10-14/html/2026-20971.htm) | The Eighth Circuit vacated the FTC's 2024 amended Negative Option Rule in July 2025; the FTC restored the earlier rule in February 2026. Do not cite the vacated rule as an operative nationwide requirement. ROSCA and applicable state law remain relevant. | Attorney before public legal claims about cancellation. |
| California renewal | [California Attorney General's 2025 summary](https://www.oag.ca.gov/news/press-releases/attorney-general-bonta-issues-consumer-alert-california%E2%80%99s-automatic-renewal-law) and the statute linked there | The amended law, effective July 1, 2025, requires express affirmative consent, cancellation information, and an online cancellation route for consumers enrolled online; it also includes acknowledgment and reminder duties whose timing depends on the offer. SafeScan's checkout and portal configuration need verification, not just a button label. | Attorney reviews offer-specific disclosures, reminders, and acknowledgment. |
| Florida renewal | [Florida Statutes § 501.165](https://www.leg.state.fl.us/statutes/index.cfm?App_mode=Display_Statute&URL=0500-0599/0501/Sections/0501.165.html) | This statute defines a covered automatic renewal provision using a renewal period longer than one month and a resulting contract term beyond six months. Do not assume its particular requirements apply to SafeScan's stated monthly plan, or that it is the only Florida law relevant to billing. | Attorney assesses actual plan and customer location. |
| Stripe cancellation capability | [Stripe customer portal support](https://support.stripe.com/questions/billing-customer-portal?locale=en-GB); [Stripe Checkout and Payment Links settings](https://docs.stripe.com/payments/checkout/limit-subscriptions) | Stripe can host subscription management and cancellation, but SafeScan must enable/configure the portal and link customers to the correct subscription. Verify the live path with a test subscription before promising “cancel in Profile.” | Owner verifies Stripe account settings; engineering tests the full path. |
| Security and privacy claims | [FTC advertising-substantiation policy](https://www.ftc.gov/legal-library/browse/ftc-policy-statement-regarding-advertising-substantiation); [FTC Start with Security guide](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/start-security-guide-business) | Objective claims, including implied safety or detection claims, need a reasonable evidentiary basis before publication. Disclosures about data handling and security must match actual behavior. “SAFE” should be treated as a product claim; change it to a bounded check result only when the result state and provider coverage support that copy. | Attorney reviews material consumer-facing claims; engineering records test evidence. |
| California privacy and GPC | [California Privacy Protection Agency's 2026 CCPA text and regulations](https://cppa.ca.gov/regulations/pdf/ccpa_statute_eff_20260101.pdf); [California DOJ CCPA overview](https://oag.ca.gov/privacy/ccpa) | Applicability depends on business thresholds and operations. For a covered business that sells or shares personal information, an opt-out preference signal such as GPC must be processed as an opt-out; the regulations expressly say this is not required for a business that does not sell or share. Do not tell users SafeScan “honors GPC” until a real data-flow and behavior audit supports that statement. | Attorney determines coverage and sale/share status; engineering maps data flows. |
| California privacy requests | [California Privacy Protection Agency FAQ](https://cppa.ca.gov/faq.html); [2026 CCPA regulations](https://cppa.ca.gov/regulations/pdf/ccpa_statute_eff_20260101.pdf) | For covered CCPA requests to delete, correct, or know, the regulations describe confirmation within 10 business days and a substantive response within 45 calendar days, with a possible explained extension. A 45-day internal target is useful, but SafeScan should not describe it as a universal legal deadline for every state or request. | Attorney sets jurisdiction-specific deadlines and exceptions. |
| Florida breach notice | [Florida Statutes § 501.171](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2026/501.171) | The statute generally requires notice to affected Florida individuals no later than 30 days after breach determination, subject to listed delay/waiver provisions, and notice to the state department for breaches affecting at least 500 Florida individuals. An incident playbook needs fact finding, evidence preservation, escalation, and attorney review; do not publish a universal 30-day rule. | Attorney decides reportability and multi-state deadlines. |
| Biometric identifiers | [Illinois BIPA definition](https://www.ilga.gov/legislation/ilcs/fulltext?DocName=074000140K10); [Texas Business and Commerce Code ch. 503](https://statutes.capitol.texas.gov/?artSec=503.001&chapter=BC.503&code=BC&tab=1); [Washington RCW 19.375.010](https://app.leg.wa.gov/RCW/default.aspx?Cite=19.375.010) | Illinois excludes ordinary photographs from “biometric identifier” but includes a face-geometry scan. Texas addresses records of face geometry; Washington's definition focuses on identifying measurements and has a photograph exclusion. Displaying a profile photo is different from extracting a face template. Keep face recognition and measurement out of QR processing and do not claim that every image is exempt from privacy law. | Attorney if face-processing features are ever proposed. |
| Accessibility | [DOJ ADA web guidance](https://www.ada.gov/resources/web-guidance/); [W3C WCAG 2.2](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/) | DOJ says websites of businesses open to the public must be accessible, but its Title III guidance does not prescribe one detailed technical standard. WCAG 2.2 AA is an appropriate engineering target, not a universal statutory certification. Automated checks cannot replace keyboard, screen-reader, and mobile testing. | Attorney reviews any accessibility statement; engineering logs known gaps. |
| PyMuPDF | [PyMuPDF licensing documentation](https://pymupdf.readthedocs.io/en/latest/about.html); [GNU AGPLv3 text](https://www.gnu.org/licenses/agpl-3.0.en.html) | PyMuPDF is offered under AGPL or a commercial license; this checkout pins `PyMuPDF==1.26.7`. AGPL obligations may affect a network service and proprietary distribution, but the exact combined-work and deployment analysis needs license counsel. Replace it with a compatible library or obtain a commercial license before asserting that the dependency is cleared. | Owner chooses license or replacement; attorney resolves license interpretation. |
| PDF replacement and notices | [pypdfium2 upstream licensing](https://github.com/pypdfium2-team/pypdfium2/blob/main/README.md); [Orbitron OFL text](https://github.com/google/fonts/blob/main/ofl/orbitron/OFL.txt); [SIL OFL FAQ](https://openfontlicense.org/ofl-faq/) | `pypdfium2` is a candidate with Apache-2.0/BSD-3-Clause choices, while its PDFium binary carries additional third-party notices. Orbitron is OFL-1.1 with a reserved font name; self-hosting is allowed, and redistribution must carry required notices. This is not a completed dependency audit: inspect the exact wheel, bundled PDFium notices, vendored assets, and all runtime packages. | Engineering inventories exact artifacts; attorney resolves unknown or conflicting licenses. |

## Research limits and decisions still needed

- The plan's statement that SafeScan is a US business, its legal entity, customer jurisdictions, audience, revenue, and mailing address were **not independently verified** in this research. Those facts govern applicability and cannot be inferred from the repository.
- The plan's proposed **18+** gate for payments, wallets, token functions, and Go Ghost is a product risk control. The sources above do not establish that 18 is a universal statutory minimum for those features. Token distribution, sanctions, payments, and regional age rules need specialist counsel before launch or expanded marketing.
- No legal position is taken here on California website-tracking class actions, a 50-state privacy or breach matrix, international targeting, defamation, trademarks, or crypto distribution. They require separate factual and legal review. Avoid turning these open questions into user-facing compliance claims.
- A feature's UI text must match deployed behavior. In particular, verify provider coverage before describing a scan result, verify Stripe cancellation before promising it, and verify email headers and suppression before enabling marketing mail.
